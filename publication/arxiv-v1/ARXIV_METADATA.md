## Title

Opposition as Local Structure in Open English WordNet: A Corpus-Scale Test of Linguistical Symmetry Theory

## Authors

Parker Rowen

## Primary category

`cs.CL` — Computation and Language

## Abstract

This paper tests a bounded version of Linguistical Symmetry Theory: the hypothesis that explicit lexical opposition can supply local semantic axes while synonymy supplies neighborhoods around their poles. A provenance-aware pipeline ingests the core 2025 release of Open English WordNet at the level of words, senses, synsets, and typed relations. The resulting graph contains 135,969 lexical entries, 185,129 senses, 107,519 synsets, and 355,064 directed relation records. It contains 3,994 unique direct antonym pairs after reciprocal directions are deduplicated. Direct opposition touches 7,774 senses (4.20%) and 6,626 distinct lemmas (5.20%). The antonym graph is strongly local: it divides into 3,838 connected components, 1,914 pairs have singleton synonym neighborhoods at both poles, and the largest component contains only four endpoints. Nevertheless, 1,040 antonym pairs have multi-lemma synonym neighborhoods at both poles; among these nontrivial cases, 584 (56.15%) have equal neighborhood sizes and the mean size-balance ratio is 0.821. The originating five lexical families are present at eleven sense-specific instantiations. The results support a corrected claim: opposition is a recurring local structure in the selected lexical resource, not a global backbone of English. The same sense graph can generate both an alphabetical lexical index and an opposition-first conceptual index while retaining polysemy, asymmetry, and unpaired material.
